## 2026-11-15 — Veiligheid layers, sticky Ranglijst, area modal (v0.17.0)

- **Why Veiligheid showed one item.** The Layers dropdown has been generated from `DATASETS` / `DATASET_GROUPS` since v0.13.0 (`groupDatasets` in app.js, no hardcoded list), and the live site serves that code with all five crime layers. Two things hid them: (1) the sub-layers are Rotterdam-only (`scope: 'rotterdam'`, police 47018NED has no national counterpart), so in the Nederland view CSS hides them and Veiligheid holds only the total; the scope is stored in localStorage, so a visitor who once picked Nederland stays there; (2) the dropdown was one scrolling column of `min(70vh, 34rem)` and Veiligheid sat below its fold. Fix: two columns from 720 px wide (a section never splits) and a line "+N lagen per wijk/buurt in de Rotterdam-weergave" under a mixed section in the Nederland view. Not verified on the operator's own browser which of the two it was.
- **Six more police categories** (47018NED, keys read from `/SoortMisdrijf` on 15 Nov 2026, Rotterdam 2025 totals in brackets): Straatroof 1.4.6 (296), Bedreiging 1.4.4 (2.154), Diefstal uit/vanaf motorvoertuigen 1.2.1 (3.906), Winkeldiefstal 2.5.2 (2.236), Vernieling cq. zaakbeschadiging 2.2.1 (3.819), Drugshandel 3.1.1 (942). Left out: 2.1.1 Drugs/drankoverlast (nuisance reports, not offences of one kind), 1.2.2 diefstal van motorvoertuigen and the environmental 2.6.x keys (small or not neighbourhood-related). Same batched query per level (one flat `or` list; a nested `or` makes the API answer 500): 15 kinds × 2 years = 2.730 buurt rows, under the 10.000-row cap.
- **A kind may feed several layers.** Straatroof and Bedreiging stay inside Geweldsmisdrijven (1.4.1–1.4.7) and are also their own layer, so `aggregateCrime` maps a key to a list of layers and the URL asks each key once.
- Each new layer: `higherIsBetter: false`, `inOverall: false`, `scope: 'rotterdam'`, a `countKey` for the Absoluut mode, full Uitleg metadata, and a panel row. `crimeRate` is labelled "Misdrijven totaal" in the dropdown.
- **Ranglijst sticky cells.** The cells already had opaque backgrounds; what showed through was the value span inside each data cell (`.list-val`, `position: relative; z-index: 1`), which painted over the sticky cells of the same z-index because it comes later in the DOM, plus the translucent flash colour. Data cells are now their own stacking context (`z-index: 0`), sticky body cells 2, header row 3, header corner 4; zebra, hover, selected and the flash keyframes are opaque (the flash colour is the old rgba flattened onto the row), and the score column casts a soft right-edge shadow. A style test rejects rgba backgrounds in `.list-table` rules.
- **A map click opens the area modal** (operator's request, replaces the first plan of a panel with a "Details" button). In Rotterdam scope a click on a wijk/buurt opens the modal directly; hover tooltips are unchanged. The side panel stays for compare and for the Nederland scope (provincie/gemeente clicks behave as before). Compare starts from the modal's "Vergelijk": the area is pinned and shown in panel slot A, and while an area is pinned the next map click on another area fills slot B instead of opening the modal (`mapClickAction` in rotterdam.js, tested). Deep links, search and "Toon op kaart" still zoom and show the quick panel. The panel keeps its "Details" button. Ranglijst rows and dashboard bars/slices open the modal too.
- **Modal "Vergelijk"** goes to the map, opens the area in slot A and pins it (clearing an older compare), so the next map click fills slot B. Handled in app.js through the `area-compare` event, because the pin state lives in rotterdam.js/panel.js.
- **Rank wording** (`modules/area-detail.js`, pure, `tests/area-detail.test.js`): peers are all areas of the same level with a value (nulls excluded, so "van 76" can be less than the 91 buurten); ties share a place (competition ranking). The line counts from the nearer end of the range, with per-dataset Dutch words: crime low end "veiligste buurt", high end "minst veilige buurt"; WOZ "hoogste/laagste WOZ-waarde"; neutral data "meeste/minste …". A middle tie speaks from the good end (neutral: the high end). In Absoluut mode crime reads "meeste/minste misdrijven", never "veiligste", because a raw count says nothing about safety per resident. The table's Rang column is the Ranglijst rank (1 = best; neutral 1 = highest).
- **"Opvallend"** = top or bottom 10 % (at least place 1) of at least 5 peers, sorted by relative position. Chips are green or red for judged datasets (good or bad end), purple for neutral ones.
- **Reference value = peer median**, not a population-weighted city average: the median of the 91 buurten (or wijken) is what the rank and percentile are measured against, and it is not pulled by the outliers. Percentages differ in procentpunten, everything else in % of the median.
- The explain dialog sits above the area modal (z-index 2600 vs 2500), since the modal's (i) buttons open it.

- **One pure module `modules/scale.js`:** `buildScale(values, { method })` returns `colorFor`, `classOf`, `isOutlier`/`outlierSide`, `classes`, `breaks`, `median`, `q1`, `q3`, `lo`/`hi` (fences) and `outliers` counts. Map (Rotterdam wijk/buurt and the Nederland tiers), legend, Ranglijst bars/chips and dashboard bars/dots all use it, built from exactly the set on screen (scope, level/tier and crime mode), so every toggle rebuilds it. `interpolateColor` moved there (re-exported by overlays.js), as did `NO_DATA_COLOR` (re-exported by rotterdam.js).
- **Default 'quantile': 7 stepped classes** on the existing purple→orange ramp, thresholds at the 1/7…6/7 quantiles (type-7 interpolation), so classes are evenly filled and the median lands in the middle class. Stepped classes read better on a map than a continuous ramp and can be listed in the legend. Ties never leave an empty class (empty classes are merged and the remaining colours spread over the whole ramp); ≤ 7 distinct values get one class per value; a constant set is one middle-colour class. **'linear'** keeps the old continuous ramp for comparison.
- **Outliers: Tukey fences (1.5·IQR) on the raw values and, when nothing is negative, on log1p(values); an outlier must be outside both** (the wider fence per side). Raw Tukey alone flags 11 of 76 buurten for crime per 1.000 (everything above ~130, a normal right tail); log alone flags the lowest buurt (23) as a low outlier, because log stretches small values. Together they flag the 6 public-space buurten (Zuiderpark 349, Charlois Zuidrand 449, Cs Kwartier 471, Cool 502, Dijkzigt 509, Zuidplein 738; fence 182) and, at wijk level, Rotterdam Centrum (231). Absolute counts and density flag nothing (buurten with 0 crimes widen the log fence; the quantile classes already handle the spread). A raw IQR of 0 or ≤ 7 distinct values flags nothing. Outliers keep their class fill (the top or bottom class) and add a solid red border `#ef4444`, weight 2.5: solid reads better than dashed on small buurt polygons over the dark basemap. Outlier features are brought to front so neighbours' strokes do not cover the border; hover still turns the stroke orange and mouseout restores the red border. At buurt zoom the wijk layer (fill off) shows no outlier borders.
- **Legend:** one row per class "from–to" (short nl-NL numbers via `formatTick`, which carries €, % and km), the median's class marked "◂ mediaan", a line "mediaan <value with full unit>" (the unit for per-1.000, misdrijven and per km² is given there rather than on every row, to keep the legend narrow), "N uitschieters > fence" with a red-bordered swatch (hidden when there are none), and "geen data". The linear method shows the gradient strip with min, max and a median tick. "Schaal: Kwantiel | Lineair" sits in the legend, is stored in localStorage `roffatistics.scale` (not in the URL, like the rank mode) and is announced with the document event `scale-method-change`, on which the map/legend, Ranglijst and Dashboard re-render.

Outliers get a small red dot after the value and the cell title says "uitschieter". The score chip uses a quantile scale over the scores. **Dashboard:** bar and scatter colours come from the scale over the 21 wijken; outlier bars get a red outline, outlier dots a red stroke.

## 2026-11-15 — Ranglijst view

- **Score = percentile rank 0–100 within the shown list** (Rotterdam wijken, buurten, provinces or gemeenten), ties share the mid-rank; one value or all-equal scores 50. `higherIsBetter: false` inverts it, `null` (population, 65+, herkomst) is shown with a heat bar but left out of the overall score. Overall = mean of the available scores; "2/3" = scores used / scored datasets that have data anywhere in the list. Logic in `modules/ranking.js` (pure, `tests/ranking.test.js`).
- **Ranks use competition ranking** (1-2-2-4). Rows with no overall score (no data, or only neutral datasets) are unranked ("—") and sink to the bottom, sorted by name. Missing values sort last in both directions.
- **Columns appear only for datasets with at least one value** in the list, so `crimeRate` shows up by itself once wijk crime data lands; hidden datasets are named in the note above the table.
- **"Rangschik op"** defaults to Totaalscore. Picking a dataset sets `state.dataset` (source `list`); later dataset changes from the map are followed only while a dataset (not Totaalscore) is selected. Ranking on a neutral dataset orders by raw value, high first.
- **Sticky columns:** rank, name and score are sticky on the left at fixed widths (CSS variables, narrower under 640 px); the dataset columns scroll horizontally inside `.list-table-wrap`, never the page.
- **Nederland scope** ranks provinces (default) or gemeenten once their CBS rows are loaded; `app.js` passes them with `setNationalRows()`. Names come from `getAreaIndex()`. Without rows the view offers a switch to Rotterdam.
- **Search contract:** `highlightArea(code)` (modules/list.js) awaits the current render, switches level when the code is a buurt/wijk (or gemeente/province) on the other level, scrolls the row to the centre (`behavior: 'auto'` under reduced motion) and flashes `.is-flash` for 2.2 s. The list also does this for `state.area` changes from other sources while visible.
## 2026-11-15 — Area search

- **Own matcher, no dependency** (`modules/fuzzy.js`, pure). Names and queries fold to lowercase ASCII letters and digits (NFKD, diacritics stripped); punctuation, apostrophes and hyphens only mark word edges, so "s-gravenhage", "'s Gravenhage" and "zuidholland" all match. Tiers: exact 1000 > prefix 900 > word-start 700 > substring 500 > one typo 300 (whole word) / 250 (half-typed word) > subsequence 100–150. Typos (Damerau/OSA distance ≤ 1 over all query tokens) only for queries of 4+ characters; substring needs 2+, subsequence 3+.
- **Ties:** inside a tier shorter names rank first, except when the query ends on a word boundary ("kralingen" in "Kralingen West"): then no length penalty, and the level order decides. Level order follows the scope: Rotterdam scope wijk > buurt > gemeente > provincie, Nederland scope gemeente > provincie > wijk > buurt.
- **Aliases** for gemeenten/provincies live in `ALIASES` (Den Haag, The Hague, Den Bosch, Friesland, …); an alias hit shows as "'s-Gravenhage (Den Haag)" with the alias highlighted.
- **Results are labelled, not regrouped:** each row carries its level ("Wijk · Rotterdam", "Buurt · Rotterdam · <wijk>", "Gemeente", "Provincie") so the list keeps one ranking and arrow keys walk it in order. At most 12 rows.
- **Selection goes through the state bus** with source `search`: Rotterdam areas → `{scope:'rotterdam', area}`, gemeenten/provincies → `{scope:'nederland', area}`, GM0599 → `{scope:'rotterdam', area:null}` plus a fit to the city. Dashboard view switches to the map first (one frame later the zoom runs on a visible map). List view calls `highlightArea(code)` from `modules/list.js` via a guarded dynamic import, 400 ms later when the scope changed so the list can re-render. Re-selecting the current area clears `area` first so the zoom still runs.
- **Nederland focus** = `focusNederlandArea(code)` in `modules/overlays.js`: fits a provincie below the gemeente tier and a gemeente on the gemeente tier (zoom 10–12), then opens its panel. `app.js` calls it on an `area` change in Nederland scope and after a scope switch to Nederland, so `#scope=nederland&area=GM0344` deep links now zoom too.
- **Recent searches:** last 5 picks in `localStorage` (`roffatistics.recentSearches`), shown when the input is empty; storage errors are ignored.

## 2026-11-15 — Woonscore (Epic W1, v0.18.0)

- **Engine** `modules/score.js` (pure, no imports): percentile 0..1 within peers (mid-rank, same rule as `ranking.js`), preference `higher`/`lower`/`target` (Gaussian with sd = tolerance, shifted to reach exactly 0 at ±2 tolerances), `filter` flags an area (`filtered`) and never drops it, score = 100 × Σ w·f / Σ w over factors with data, null below 60 % weight coverage, `explain()` = plus/minus factors by impact 100·w·(f − 0.5)/Σw (above or below the peer middle).
- **log1p:** a percentile rank is unchanged by a monotone transform, so log1p before the percentile (plan §1) changes nothing; the `log` flag is kept and matters only for `target` distances. Shrinkage works on raw rates.
- **Shrinkage** only for buurten (parent = wijk) and only on rates per 1.000 (`RATE_KEYS`: crime, bijstand): rate' = w·rate + (1 − w)·wijk, w = n/(n + 300), applied to every buurt (proper empirical Bayes; large buurten barely move). The "weinig inwoners" badge shows when n < 300, i.e. when the wijk weighs more than the buurt. Wijken and NL gemeenten are not shrunk. The shown values stay the raw rates; only the score uses the shrunk ones.
- **Standaard preset** (`modules/presets.js`): a theme weight (35/20/25/10/10) is split evenly over the theme's metrics that have data among the peers; a theme without data is dropped and the rest renormalise. So NL gemeenten (crimeRate, avgIncome, highEdu) get veiligheid 35/65, welvaart 20/65, opleiding 10/65 instead of the crime metric collapsing to one fifth of 35. `ownerOccupiedPct` counts as a mild plus here although it is neutral as a layer.
- **Gelijk gewogen preset** = weight 1 for every directional `inOverall` dataset, no shrinkage, no coverage threshold; tested to equal the Ranglijst Totaalscore. The Totaalscore code (`ranking.scoreAreas`, `area-detail.overallRank`) is left as it is rather than rerouted, so its crime-mode (`abs`) behaviour and the used/possible counter stay unchanged.
- **Where the score is computed:** `areas.js` adds `values.woonscore` and the full result (`area.woonscore`) to wijken and buurten at load; `app.js` adds `woonscore` to the province and gemeente rows after each CBS merge. The Woonscore always uses rates, also in crime mode Absoluut. It is a normal dataset (`woonscore`, group "Score", unit `score`, `inOverall: false`), so map, legend, Uitleg, dashboard and Ranglijst pick it up; the Ranglijst ranks and shows the score itself (not its percentile).
- **Default layer and deep links:** `DEFAULT_STATE.dataset` is `woonscore`. The hash now carries `dataset=` when it is not the Woonscore, so a link to another layer keeps it; a hash without `dataset` opens on the Woonscore. Old links (which never had `dataset`) open on the Woonscore instead of Bevolking.
- **Not in W1:** the Ranglijst breakdown column per theme, the `#score=<preset>` hash and a preset switcher (W3).
